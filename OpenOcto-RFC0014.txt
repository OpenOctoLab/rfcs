                  OCP over Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)


Abstract

   This document specifies how the Octopus Control Protocol (OCP) is
   carried over Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE).  The binding is a GATT
   service with two characteristics and a one-octet fragment header;
   the OCP protocol above it -- the frame format, the HELLO/WELCOME
   handshake, calls, publish/subscribe, tunnels, compression and
   authentication -- is unchanged from [RFC0004].  BLE is a reliable,
   ordered transport, so no OCP retransmission or datagram mode is
   used.  The binding adds no OCP-layer encryption: confidentiality
   and integrity belong to the BLE link layer, as described in
   Section 9.

1.  Introduction

   OCP [RFC0004] makes no assumption about the octet transport: a
   binding only has to provide delimited frames and to state whether
   the transport is reliable ([RFC0004], Section 7.3).  This document
   gives it a BLE binding.

   BLE is attractive for robots and bench equipment because it needs no
   network infrastructure and because the radio link already carries
   its own link-layer security.  This binding therefore leaves all
   confidentiality, integrity and authentication of the link to BLE.
   It does not use the frame-layer security of [RFC0009], and it is not
   a secure transport in the sense of [RFC0010].  A program selects it
   with the "ocpb" URI scheme and otherwise sees the ordinary OCP
   session of [RFC0004].

   The key words MUST, MUST NOT, REQUIRED, SHALL, SHALL NOT, SHOULD,
   SHOULD NOT, RECOMMENDED, NOT RECOMMENDED, MAY and OPTIONAL in this
   document are to be interpreted as described in [RFC0001], Section 4.

2.  Conventions

   "ATT" is the Attribute Protocol, "GATT" the Generic Attribute
   Profile, "ATT_MTU" the negotiated Attribute Protocol maximum
   transmission unit, and "central" and "peripheral" are the GATT roles
   of [BLUETOOTH].  "Initiator" and "responder" are the OCP roles of
   [RFC0004].

   An "OCP frame" is the complete frame of [RFC0004], Section 3.1:
   header, payload and optional trailer.  A "GATT value" is the value of
   one characteristic notification or write.  A "fragment" is one GATT
   value carrying part of one OCP frame.

3.  The Model

   A BLE GATT connection carries exactly one OCP session.  It is one
   peer pair, exactly as one TCP connection is one peer pair in
   [RFC0004], Section 7.1.  There is no connection or session identifier
   in the binding and no multiplexing: a peer that needs several
   sessions opens several BLE connections, or uses tunnels for
   sub-streams.

   The GATT central is the OCP initiator: it connects, discovers the
   service, subscribes and sends HELLO.  The GATT peripheral is the OCP
   responder: it advertises the service and answers HELLO with WELCOME.
   This is a mapping of the usual client/server roles and not a
   restriction on which product has which role: a phone MAY be the
   central to a robot peripheral, and a device that initiates an OCP
   session to another device follows the same rule.

   The transport is a reliable, ordered stream of octets after
   reassembly (Section 6).  It is therefore bound by the stream rules of
   [RFC0004], Section 7.1: frames are written back to back with no
   padding, and a receiver parses by the length in the frame header.
   The datagram rules of [RFC0004], Section 7.2 do not apply: the
   F_ACK flag, ACK frames, datagram retransmission and the 1200-octet
   datagram budget are not used on this binding.

4.  Device Identification and URI Scheme

   The URI scheme of this binding is

      ocpb://<device>

   where <device> is either a Bluetooth device address in the form
   AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF (hexadecimal, case-insensitive) or a local name
   advertised by the peripheral.  There is no port: BLE has no
   transport port, and the RECOMMENDED ports of [RFC0004] and
   [RFC0010] do not apply.

   The URI scheme is used by configuration files, tools and logs, and by
   the host language's connect() ([RFC0003], Section 9.2).  It is a
   configuration convention, not part of the wire protocol: neither
   endpoint parses it at run time.

   A peripheral MUST advertise the OCP service UUID of Section 5 in its
   advertising data or scan response, so that a central can filter its
   scan by service.  A central MAY also match a device by address or
   local name; a deployment SHOULD prefer the address or a deployment-
   assigned name over a generic advertised name shared by many devices.

   BLE privacy addresses rotate.  A deployment that identifies devices
   by address SHOULD use a stable identity address, or resolve the
   current address out of band; a rotating address is not a stable
   identity and an OCP authentication exchange (Section 9) is what
   establishes identity when it matters.

5.  The GATT Service

   The binding defines one primary service and two characteristics.

   +====================+===============================================+
   |Item                |UUID                                           |
   +====================+===============================================+
   |OCP service         |6f63702d-626c-6500-8000-000000000000           |
   +--------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
   |OCP -> Device       |6f63702d-626c-6500-8000-000000000001           |
   |(host writes)       |                                               |
   +--------------------+-----------------------------------------------+
   |Device -> OCP       |6f63702d-626c-6500-8000-000000000002           |
   |(device notifies)   |                                               |
   +--------------------+-----------------------------------------------+

                       Table 1: GATT UUIDs

   The OCP -> Device characteristic is written by the central and read by
   the peripheral.  It MUST support GATT Write (write with response).
   It MAY also support Write Without Response; a central MUST NOT use
   that weaker form for OCP (Section 8).

   The Device -> OCP characteristic is sent by the peripheral and
   received by the central.  It MUST support GATT Notify, and it MAY
   also support Indicate.  A central MUST subscribe to it before it
   sends HELLO, and MUST accept both notifications and indications.  A
   peripheral MAY use indications instead of notifications when its
   stack or the link requires an acknowledged ATT transaction; the OCP
   octets are identical either way.

   No characteristic descriptor is required.  In particular the binding
   does not use the Client Characteristic Configuration Descriptor
   directly: GATT Subscribe and Unsubscribe operations manage it.

   The service can be discovered by UUID alone; a central does not need
   to read a version characteristic or exchange an OCP-level capability
   before HELLO.  A peripheral that exposes an older version of the
   service keeps the UUIDs of this document; a changed wire format
   would need a new revision of this document.

6.  Fragmentation

   GATT delivers values whose size is bounded by ATT_MTU.  The maximum
   value length of a characteristic operation is ATT_MTU - 3 octets
   (one octet of opcode and two octets of attribute handle are outside
   the value).  One octet of every value is the fragment header, so one
   fragment carries at most ATT_MTU - 4 octets of the OCP stream.

      +--------+------------------------------------------------------+
      | Flags  | OCP octets                                           |
      | 1 octet| up to ATT_MTU - 4 octets                             |
      +--------+------------------------------------------------------+

                       Figure 1: Fragment Format

   Flags:

      bit 0 (0x01)  FIRST: this value is the first fragment of an OCP
                    frame.

      bit 1 (0x02)  LAST: this value is the last fragment of an OCP
                    frame.

      bits 2-7       Reserved.  A sender MUST set them to zero and a
                    receiver MUST reject a value in which any is set.

   A frame that fits one value has both FIRST and LAST set in its only
   fragment.  A frame that spans several values has FIRST on the first,
   LAST on the last, and neither on the middle fragments.  One value
   carries exactly one fragment of exactly one frame; a sender MUST NOT
   mix two frames in one value and MUST NOT put a frame boundary inside
   a value.

   The fragments of a frame are sent in order.  Because the BLE link
   layer is reliable and ordered (Section 8), a receiver treats a
   missing or out-of-order fragment as a broken transport, not as a
   lost frame.  It MUST close the connection and fail the OCP session;
   it MUST NOT attempt an OCP-level retransmission.

   A receiver assembles the OCP octets of the fragments between FIRST
   and LAST and then parses the frame exactly as [RFC0004] requires.
   The OCP frame header's declared length is authoritative: a fragment
   group whose assembled octets do not contain exactly the frame the
   header declares is malformed.  A frame larger than the receiver's
   maximum is an error and the connection is closed; implementations
   SHOULD use the OCP max_payload negotiation of [RFC0004] to keep
   frames within their capacity, but fragmentation makes the binding
   independent of ATT_MTU.

   A sender SHOULD fill every non-final fragment to the full value
   capacity (ATT_MTU - 3 octets) and SHOULD use one GATT write or
   notification per fragment, so that a small control frame is not
   delayed behind a tunnel's fragments.  A frame MAY be fragmented even
   when it would fit one value; the FIRST/LAST bits, not the size,
   define the grouping.

   Worked example: ATT_MTU 247, value capacity 244, fragment data
   capacity 243.  A 500-octet frame is three fragments: FIRST with 243
   octets, a middle fragment with 243 octets, and LAST with 14 octets.

7.  Session Establishment and Lifecycle

   The central:

   1.  scans for the service UUID (or for a known address/name);
   2.  connects and discovers the service and its two characteristics;
   3.  subscribes to the Device -> OCP characteristic;
   4.  sends OCP HELLO on the OCP -> Device characteristic, fragmented
       as Section 6 requires; and
   5.  pumps the session as [RFC0004], Section 6.1 specifies.

   The peripheral advertises the service, accepts a connection,
   subscribes nothing (it is the notifier), and answers HELLO with
   WELCOME.  The OCP handshake is the only handshake: there is no
   transport-level negotiation, no ALPN analogue, and no BLE-level
   "OCP version" exchange.  A peer that cannot speak this binding simply
   does not expose the service, and the central does not send HELLO to
   it.

   A BLE disconnection ends the OCP session.  The same rule as a closed
   TCP stream applies: the session is dead, outstanding calls fail with
   SessionExpired (or the mapped transport error), and a later
   connect() opens a new session with a new HELLO.  There is no
   resumption and no implicit reconnection at the binding layer; the
   host language MAY make the reconnection decision of [RFC0003],
   Section 9.2.

   The BLE ATT_MTU and the OCP max_payload are independent: ATT_MTU
   bounds a fragment, and OCP max_payload bounds a frame's payload as
   [RFC0004], Section 3.3 and [RFC0011], Section 10 specify.
   Fragmentation lets any legal OCP frame run over any legal ATT_MTU.
   A responder that already has a small OCP max_payload (an Air peer,
   for example) keeps it unchanged.

8.  Reliability, Ordering and Flow Control

   The BLE link layer delivers data packets reliably and in order.  This
   binding therefore uses OCP's stream mode, not its datagram mode:
   ACK frames and F_ACK are not used, and the session is not configured
   for datagram retransmission.

   The central uses GATT Write (write with response) for every fragment
   it sends.  The ATT response is the sender's flow-control credit: at
   most one write is outstanding, and a failed write ends the session.
   A central MUST NOT use Write Without Response for OCP.

   The peripheral sends one notification or indication per fragment.
   ATT's notification flow control and the BLE controller's buffers
   bound how far it may run ahead.  A peripheral MUST NOT drop an OCP
   fragment while a central is subscribed: a stack that cannot queue a
   notification uses an indication, which is acknowledged, or closes the
   connection.  A central MUST keep pumping; one that stops reading
   eventually causes the peripheral's queue to fill, and the peripheral
   MAY then disconnect rather than lose a fragment.

   The binding adds no sequence number and no acknowledgement of its
   own.  A fragment lost in the link layer is a link failure, surfaced
   as a disconnected session; it is never repaired by OCP.

9.  Security Considerations

   This binding provides no OCP-layer confidentiality, integrity or
   authentication.  It MUST NOT use the frame-layer security of
   [RFC0009] (F_ENC, F_ITG, F_ZTR, the extended header or a frame
   secret), and it is not a secure transport under [RFC0010]: there is
   no TLS, DTLS or QUIC underneath, no "ocp/1" ALPN and no exporter for
   the "tls-exporter" channel binding.  A program that asks connect()
   for either on ocpb:// is refused rather than silently downgraded.

   Confidentiality and integrity, when required, are the BLE link's:
   the deployment SHOULD use an encrypted and authenticated link (LE
   Security Connections pairing, or an equivalent link-layer policy)
   and SHOULD require it before exposing the OCP service to writes.
   The binding cannot verify that policy on the wire; it is a
   deployment responsibility, and an endpoint that needs a proof SHOULD
   use OCP authentication (the "token" method of [RFC0004], Section
   6.2) in addition to, never instead of, link security.  The "hmac"
   method is not used on this binding because it also yields the
   keying material of [RFC0009], which Section 9 above excludes.

   An address or local name is an identifier, not an identity: a nearby
   device can advertise the same name and a privacy address can rotate.
   The security of a deployment therefore comes from BLE pairing and
   link security and, when a peer must be identified, from an OCP
   authentication exchange -- not from the text in the URI.

   The BLE service exposes no unauthenticated administrative surface:
   its only writable characteristic carries OCP frames, and every OCP
   rule of [RFC0004] applies to them.

10. Error Mapping

   A GATT error, a disconnected link, a malformed fragment or a
   reassembly failure is a transport failure, not an OCP protocol
   error.  The host closes the session and reports the failure the way
   it reports a closed TCP stream: outstanding calls fail with
   SessionExpired or the host's transport-error kind, and a later
   connect() starts a new session.

   This binding defines no new OCP error codes.  The OCP codes of
   [RFC0004], Appendix A remain the only errors on the wire; an ERROR
   frame is carried and processed exactly as on any other transport.

11. IANA Considerations

   This document requests no IANA action.  The 128-bit UUIDs of
   Section 5 are assigned by the deployment or the product; they are
   not IANA registrations.  The "ocpb" URI scheme is a configuration
   convention and is not registered with IANA.  There is no port to
   assign.
