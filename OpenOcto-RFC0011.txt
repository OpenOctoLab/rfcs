                   OCP Air: an OCP Subset for MCUs

Abstract

   This document defines "OCP Air", a constrained subset of the Octopus
   Control Protocol [RFC0004] that fits a microcontroller-class device
   such as an STM32 (tens of kilobytes of RAM, no required operating
   system).  Air is not a fork and not a new protocol version: an Air
   device speaks OCP version 1 and interoperates with a full OCP stack
   by the capability intersection that [RFC0004] already defines.  This
   document repeats no protocol detail; it states only what to keep,
   what to drop, and what to make optional, with the resource reasoning
   behind each choice.

1.  Introduction

   An STM32-class microcontroller can run OCP, but it cannot run all of
   [RFC0004].  Three parts of that document are excluded by an Air
   device by policy, not by inability to parse them: they are simply too
   large for the device's RAM or for its threat model.

   *  Compression (Section 3.5, 3.6).  zstd, brotli, DEFLATE and friends
      need windows of tens of kilobytes and dynamic structures an MCU
      rarely has room for.  Air drops compression entirely.

   *  Frame-layer security [RFC0009].  Its longer header, per-frame
      cryptography and key schedule are out of scope for Air.

   *  Secure transport [RFC0010].  TLS, DTLS and QUIC each need a TCP/IP
      stack plus a cryptography suite measured in tens of kilobytes; Air
      does not use them.

   Everything else in [RFC0004] is kept, sometimes with a smaller bound.
   Air is therefore a declared profile: a full OCP peer that meets an
   Air device negotiates the intersection and proceeds exactly as the
   core document allows.

2.  Conventions

   The key words of [IETF-RFC2119] and [IETF-RFC8174] apply within this
   document to the Air profile.  All other rules are those of [RFC0004];
   this document changes none of them.  Multi-octet integers are
   big-endian.  "Air device" means an implementation that claims
   conformance to this profile.

3.  Scope: What Air Keeps and Drops

   Table 1 summarises every area of [RFC0004] and the Air decision.  A
   "Keep" row is implemented as written in [RFC0004].  A "Drop" row is
   not implemented by an Air device.  An "Optional" row MAY be left out
   to save code and RAM; when present it follows [RFC0004] unchanged.

   +================+=========+========================================+
   |Area            |Decision |Note                                    |
   +================+=========+========================================+
   |Frame header    |Keep     |16-octet form; header_len=0x0010        |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |Frame security  |Drop     |RFC0009 not in Air                      |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |Compression     |Drop     |F_COMP, algs; too heavy                 |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |Authentication  |Optional |pre-shared "token" only                 |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |CBOR decoder    |Keep     |subset of types (Section 4)             |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |HELLO/WELCOME   |Keep     |no auth/algs members                    |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |CALL/RESULT     |Keep     |core RPC                                |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |EVENT/SUB/UNSUB |Keep     |pubsub                                  |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |TUNDATA/TUNSTAT |Optional |blobtun; needs RAM                      |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |ACK/F_ACK       |Drop     |stream transport only                   |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |Capabilities    |Keep     |rpc+pubsub min; blobtun opt             |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |Core profile    |Keep     |7 instructions (Section 9)              |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |Topic spaces    |Keep     |device.* and core.*                     |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |Stream transport|Keep     |UART/USB/TCP baseline                   |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |Datagram trans. |Optional |UDP; needs ACK logic                    |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   |Error codes     |Keep     |0x0000-0x000E; reused                   |
   +----------------+---------+----------------------------------------+

                     Table 1: Air Scope Decisions

   An Air device MUST NOT implement the frame security of [RFC0009] or
   the transports of [RFC0010]; doing so is a different product, not
   Air.  It MUST NOT set F_COMP (Section 4 of [RFC0004]).

4.  Frame Layer Reductions

   Air keeps the sixteen-octet header of [RFC0004] Section 3.1 exactly,
   including the magic octets and "header_len" (always 0x0010).  These
   fields cost nothing and let the receiver find the payload before it
   knows anything else, which is exactly what a small parser wants.

   *  Flag bits.  Only the header form of [RFC0004] is used.  F_COMP
      (0x02) is never set.  F_AUTH (0x04) is permitted only together
      with the authentication option of Section 7.  F_ACK (0x01) is not
      used, because Air runs on a reliable stream (Section 9).

   *  Trailers and longer headers.  None.  [RFC0009] is not used, so the
      extension points of [RFC0004] Section 3.8 are inert for Air.

   *  Sequence numbers.  Kept as written.  The 32-bit counter and the
      serial-number arithmetic are cheap; an Air device MAY close a
      long-lived session well before 2^31 frames, which [RFC0004]
      Section 3.4 already permits.

   *  Limits.  Kept, but with a small bound, see Section 10.

5.  Value Layer Reductions: a CBOR Subset

   Air keeps the one-value-per-frame rule and the member rules of
   [RFC0004] Section 4.  To keep the decoder small, an Air device
   implements a CBOR subset rather than the standard [IETF-RFC8949].

   *  Types an Air decoder MUST accept: unsigned integer, negative
      integer, byte string, text string, array, map, boolean, null.

   *  Floating point: OPTIONAL.  Measurements on an MCU are usually
      integers or fixed-point; an Air device that omits floats answers
      E_BAD_PAYLOAD to a float item.  An Air device that accepts floats
      decodes the binary64 (double) form, as [RFC0004] Section 4.4
      recommends.  A device that omits floats MUST NOT be sent one, and
      one thing follows for the heartbeat of [RFC0004] Section 6.7: it
      carries the time as a whole number of seconds, and a responder
      answers in the form it was asked in, so a heartbeat between two
      such devices needs no float in either direction.  A device that
      has no clock to report answers with the echoed time alone, because
      a clock cannot be invented.

   *  Numeric width: Air uses the same default widths as [RFC0004]
      Section 4.4 -- integers at 32-bit signed and floats at [IEEE754]
      binary64.  The two members of the family are therefore isomorphic
      at the value layer: no value legal in one is illegal in the other
      for a width reason, and a peer need not narrow a value for an Air
      device.

   *  Tags: an Air decoder MUST ignore a tag and decode the tagged data
      item directly, rather than reject it.

   *  Indefinite-length items (major types 2, 3, 4, 5 with the
      additional-information value 31): an Air decoder SHOULD reject
      them.  A definite-length-only decoder is far smaller and the core
      profile of [RFC0004] never needs indefinite length.

   *  A decoder MUST still accept a non-canonical but legal encoding, as
      [RFC0004] Section 2 requires; "subset" means fewer types, not a
      stricter encoder.

6.  Message Layer Reductions

   The message types an Air device MUST implement are the session and
   RPC/PubSub set: HELLO, WELCOME, CALL, RESULT, EVENT, SUBSCRIBE,
   UNSUBSCRIBE, BYE and ERROR (types 0x01 to 0x0B of [RFC0004] Table 5).

   *  ACK (0x0C) is dropped.  It exists for datagram transports only,
      and Air's baseline transport is a stream (Section 9).  An Air
      device that also implements the datagram option of Section 9 MAY
      add ACK and F_ACK back, unchanged.

   *  TUNDATA (0x08) and TUNSTAT (0x09) are OPTIONAL.  The blob tunnel
      needs a sliding-window buffer and retransmission state that an MCU
      may not afford; see Section 10.  An Air device that omits it
      simply does not advertise "blobtun" and never opens a tunnel.  A
      tunnel frame that arrives anyway, from a peer that ignored the
      capabilities or read the absence of "blobtun" for a fault, is
      answered with E_UNSUPPORTED (0x0008): it is not processed, and not
      discarded silently either.  The device defines no tunnel, so there
      is none to name, which is also why 0x000C has no use here.

   *  ERROR and the error registry are kept.  Air reuses the codes
      0x0000 to 0x000E of [RFC0004] Appendix A and defines none of its
      own, so a full peer reads Air errors without a new table.  Codes
      0x000C and 0x000D (tunnel) are unused when blobtun is omitted.

7.  Session, Flags and Capabilities

   Session establishment follows [RFC0004] Section 6, simplified:

   *  HELLO and WELCOME carry "proto", "client"/"server", "caps",
      "max_payload" and "flags".  The "algs" member is absent (no
      compression).  The "auth" member is absent unless the
      authentication option below is taken.

   *  "flags" is 0 in Air, or 0x04 only when authentication is used.
      There is no compression flag to negotiate.

   *  Authentication is OPTIONAL.  When an Air device authenticates, it
      uses exactly one method: "token", a pre-shared bearer ticket
      carried in the "auth" member as [RFC0004] Section 6.2 shows.  The
      mutual "hmac" method, [RFC0009] and [RFC0010] are not used.  A
      deployment that needs stronger assurance runs a full OCP stack.

   *  Capabilities: an Air device advertises at least "rpc" and
      "pubsub".  It adds "blobtun" only when it implements the tunnel.
      The effective set is the intersection, as [RFC0004] Section 8
      states, so a full peer quietly stops using what Air lacks.

   *  The abandonment timeout of [RFC0004] Section 6.3 is kept; an MCU
      cannot hold a half-open session forever.

8.  Service Profile

   Air keeps the Core Profile of [RFC0004] Section 9.1: the seven
   instructions ping, status, metadata, sense, actuate, get_param and
   set_param.  They are small, and they are exactly what an MCU device
   exposes -- read a sensor, move an actuator, read or write a setting,
   report state, and let a peer discover the rest through metadata.

   *  Topic namespaces: an Air device publishes and subscribes the
      device.* topic of [RFC0004] Table 21.  The pseudo-topics
      "published" by a client runtime ([RFC0004] Section 9.2.1) are a
      peer-side concern; an Air device MAY publish device.event,
      device.fault, device.status and device.param.

   *  Wildcards "+" and "#" in SUBSCRIBE are kept; they cost almost
      nothing and [RFC0004] Section 6.5.2 already defines them.

9.  Transport Binding for an MCU

   Air's baseline is the stream transport of [RFC0004] Section 7.1:
   frames written back to back, delimited only by "payload_len".  Good
   fits for an STM32 are a UART, a USB CDC ACM endpoint, or a TCP socket
   handed to the device by an external network module (for example an
   ESP-class Wi-Fi coprocessor in AT-passthrough mode, or an Ethernet
   PHY with a small IP stack).  None of these needs the device to run
   TLS itself.

   *  A datagram (UDP) transport is OPTIONAL.  It brings ACK, F_ACK,
      retransmission and dedup, which an MCU can do but which cost code
      and a small per-frame state.  An Air device that stays on a stream
      never implements them.

   *  The RECOMMENDED port of [RFC0004] Section 7.4 (6286) and the
      "ocp://" scheme apply unchanged; they are conventions, not wire
      content.

10. Resource Budgets

   Table 2 gives the [RFC0004] default and an Air recommendation for the
   numbers that drive RAM use.  An Air device advertises its own small
   "max_payload" in HELLO; the effective limit is the smaller of the
   two, so a full peer sends only what the MCU can buffer.

   +================+============+=====================================+
   |Resource        |RFC0004     |Air                                  |
   +================+============+=====================================+
   |max_payload     |1048576     |up to 1024 octets                    |
   +----------------+------------+-------------------------------------+
   |rx frame buf    |1048576     |1024 octets                          |
   +----------------+------------+-------------------------------------+
   |tunnel window   |65536       |omit or 2048                         |
   +----------------+------------+-------------------------------------+
   |tunnel slice    |8192        |omit or 256                          |
   +----------------+------------+-------------------------------------+
   |receive buf     |262144      |omit or 4096                         |
   +----------------+------------+-------------------------------------+
   |abandon timeout |15 s        |15-60 s                              |
   +----------------+------------+-------------------------------------+
   |ping interval   |5 s         |5-30 s                               |
   +----------------+------------+-------------------------------------+

                  Table 2: Air Resource Budgets

   With these bounds a minimal Air responder needs roughly one frame
   buffer (1 KiB), a small outstanding-call table, and the metadata it
   chooses to publish -- well within an STM32 with a few kilobytes of
   RAM.  Flash cost is dominated by the CBOR subset decoder and the
   procedure handlers the device defines; compression, TLS and the
   tunnel are the pieces that are omitted to keep it small.

11. Interoperability with Full OCP

   Air is not a separate version.  An Air device sends "proto": 1 and is
   read by any [RFC0004] peer as an ordinary OCP 1 endpoint.  Because
   the core document already treats unknown members, unknown message
   types and absent capabilities as harmless, the two interoperate by
   intersection:

   *  A full peer that offers compression sees no "algs" in Air's HELLO
      and proceeds without it.

   *  A full peer that implements blobtun but meets an Air device that
      advertises only "rpc" and "pubsub" never opens a tunnel.

   *  Error codes, sequence arithmetic and the header are identical, so
      no translation shim is needed on either side.

   The only thing an Air device cannot do is accept a session that
   requires [RFC0009] or [RFC0010]; per [RFC0004] Section 6.1 it closes
   such a session rather than downgrade silently.

12. Security Considerations

   Air carries cleartext, exactly as [RFC0004] Section 12 describes for
   a session with no extension.  It is suitable only for a controlled
   environment: a lab bench, a trusted LAN, or a link that is already
   protected by the hardware around it (a private UART, a USB wire, a
   VLAN).  An Air device MUST NOT be placed on an untrusted network
   without an outer protection that this document does not provide.

   The OPTIONAL "token" authentication proves only that the peer holds a
   shared secret; it gives no confidentiality or forward secrecy, and it
   authenticates the initiator but not (in the token method) the
   responder.  Deployments that need real confidentiality or mutual
   authentication use a full OCP stack with [RFC0009] or [RFC0010].

13. IANA Considerations

   This document requests no IANA action and defines no new registry,
   code point or name.  It reuses the message types, flag bits,
   capability names and error codes of [RFC0004] without change, and it
   adds no topic under a reserved prefix.
