                 The OpenOcto Device Descriptor Format

Abstract

   A device daemon is a virtual device: it answers the Octopus Control
   Protocol [RFC0004] on one side, talks to hardware on the other, and
   lets no caller see the difference.  A descriptor is the translation:
   a TOML document that names the buses, states how a call becomes
   octets on a bus and how received octets become named values again,
   declares the procedures, sensors, actuators, parameters and events
   the device offers, and states the sessions it will and will not
   accept.  A peer that already speaks [RFC0004] is not described and is
   not translated: it is relayed, and a descriptor carries nothing about
   its octets.  A descriptor contains no control logic: it translates,
   and what acts on what a device reports is a program and not a file.
   This document specifies that format completely: every table, every
   key, its type and its default, the rules that tie the tables to one
   another, the validation a descriptor undergoes before any of it is
   applied, and the diagnostics a violation produces.

1.  Introduction

   A device daemon of OCP [RFC0004] is a virtual device.  It answers
   calls, publishes events and moves byte streams on one side; it drives
   a bus on the other; and the two sides never meet.  A caller sees a
   device with procedures, sensors, actuators, parameters and events,
   and it sees no bus, no address and no frame.  That is the point of
   the arrangement: hardware differs between two machines that do the
   same job, and nothing about the difference reaches a program.

   A descriptor is the translation between the two sides.  It is data
   and not code: it is read once at start-up, validated as a whole, and
   then drives the daemon for as long as it runs, so that changing what
   a device does means changing a file and not a program.

   Five ideas shape the format.

   Translation is the whole job.  A descriptor says what an instruction
   means on a bus and what a frame means to a program.  It holds no
   reaction, no policy and no program, and nothing in it decides what is
   to be done about what the device reports: that decision belongs to a
   program on the far side of a session.  What acts through a descriptor
   is a caller, from outside, through an instruction.

   A bus is hardware.  It carries frames between the daemon and a
   device, this descriptor encodes and decodes them, and nothing outside
   Sections 9 to 11 knows that the bus exists.  A bus never appears in
   what the daemon publishes.

   A peer that speaks [RFC0004] is relayed and not translated.  There is
   nothing to translate: the peer already answers for itself, so the
   daemon passes the call on unchanged and passes the answer back
   unchanged.  Relaying and translating are mutually exclusive, and a
   name is either one or the other and never both (Section 8).

   One codec serves both directions.  A codec is an ordered list of
   fields, each with a name, a position and a type.  Encoding renders
   arguments into octets; decoding reads octets back into the same
   names.  The two are one list read in opposite directions, so a frame
   template and a frame filter are the same object and cannot disagree
   about what a frame means.

   One device, several ways to serve it.  The translation is one, and a
   descriptor MAY offer it on as many addresses and in as many variants
   of [RFC0004] as a deployment wants (Section 6).  A serve mode is one
   such way: it binds its own host and port, speaks its own variant of
   [RFC0004], and the daemon opens every declared serve mode in
   parallel, each from its own [serve.<name>] table, a
   sub-configuration of the descriptor.  What differs between two
   serve modes is where the device is offered, which variant is
   spoken there, and which part of the
   translation is exposed; the translation itself does not change, and
   all serve modes serve the same device from it.

   What a descriptor declares reaches a caller through the mechanisms
   [RFC0004] already defines: what the device offers is answered by the
   core instruction "metadata" (Section 18.2), a call is answered by
   translation or by a relay, and what the device reports is published
   on the topics of Section 18.3.  What it declares about a session,
   including what it requires before it will serve one, is Section 5 and
   is governed by [RFC0009] and [RFC0010].

   A descriptor is read by the device daemon of [RFC0006] and by nothing
   else: it is the daemon's translation table and not a description that
   a peer consumes or a device carries.  A device that is called
   directly, with no daemon in front of it, is not described here at
   all.  Such a device speaks [RFC0004] itself, which is to say that
   whoever puts it on the network implements an OCP-speaking control
   program on its behalf; the program that calls it then needs no
   descriptor, because there is nothing left to translate.  A program
   written in OctoScript [RFC0003] reaches what a descriptor declares
   through the instructions of [RFC0004], Section 9.1.  The architecture
   that puts the daemon, the programs and the protocol together is
   [RFC0002], and a term that this document takes from it carries the
   meaning that document gives it.

2.  Conventions

   The key words of [IETF-RFC2119] and [IETF-RFC8174] apply.  A
   descriptor is a TOML document [TOML].  The type names used in the
   tables of this document are listed in Table 1.

   +=========+=========================================================+
   |Type     |Meaning                                                  |
   +=========+=========================================================+
   |integer  |A TOML integer.                                          |
   +---------+---------------------------------------------------------+
   |float    |A TOML float; an integer is accepted.                    |
   +---------+---------------------------------------------------------+
   |boolean  |A TOML boolean.                                          |
   +---------+---------------------------------------------------------+
   |string   |A TOML string.                                           |
   +---------+---------------------------------------------------------+
   |array    |A TOML array; the element type is stated where used.     |
   +---------+---------------------------------------------------------+
   |table    |A TOML table.                                            |
   +---------+---------------------------------------------------------+
   |value    |An integer, a float or a string.                         |
   +---------+---------------------------------------------------------+
   |any      |Any of the above.                                        |
   +---------+---------------------------------------------------------+

                 Table 1: Type Names Used in This Document

   [RFC0002] and [RFC0003] are normative for this document.  A term that
   [RFC0002] defines carries the same meaning here, and the programs of
   the series, which are the callers a descriptor serves, are written in
   OctoScript [RFC0003].

   Names.  Every object of this format has a name: a session, a serve
   mode, a whitelist, a blacklist, a relay, a bus, a codec, an
   instruction, an actuator, a sensor, a parameter, a signal, an
   enumeration and a field.  A name is a non-empty string and is
   compared as exact octets.  A name SHOULD consist only of ASCII
   letters, digits and the three characters "_", "-" and "."; a name
   that contains anything else is accepted but is awkward to carry in a
   procedure name or in a topic.  The namespaces are separate, so a bus
   and an instruction MAY share a name and the key that names one says
   which is meant; two objects of the same kind MUST NOT share one.

   Order.  Where a list is declared, as the fields of a codec or the
   arguments of an instruction, the order of declaration is part of its
   meaning (Sections 11.1 and 12).  Everywhere else the order of keys
   carries no meaning, and a descriptor MUST NOT rely on it.

   Paths.  A key path is written as the TOML path of the table or the
   key it names, for example bus.main.transport.  Diagnostics are
   written with it (Section 17).

   Strictness.  A key that a table does not define is an error, in
   every table of this format and not only in some of them.  Nothing is
   accepted and then ignored: a descriptor that no one validates is a
   descriptor that no one can reason about.

3.  Document Structure

   The top-level tables are listed in Table 2.

   +======================+============================================+
   |Table                 |Purpose                                     |
   +======================+============================================+
   |[meta]                |Identity and format version of this document|
   |                      |(Section 4).                                |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[session.<name>]      |One variant of [RFC0004]: what it offers,   |
   |                      |requires and is carried by (Section 5).     |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[serve.<name>]        |One way the device is served (Section 6).   |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[[whitelist]]         |An array of allowed callers (Section 7).    |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[[blacklist]]         |An array of denied callers (Section 7).     |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[relay.<name>]        |One relay that passes calls on unchanged    |
   |                      |(Section 8).                                |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[bus.<name>]          |One hardware bus (Section 9).               |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[enum.<name>]         |A map from integers to names (Section 10).  |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[codec.<name>]        |One frame layout (Section 11).              |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[instruction.<name>]  |One procedure a program may call            |
   |                      |(Section 12).                               |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[actuator.<name>]     |One thing "actuate" operates (Section 13).  |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[sensor.<name>]       |One thing "sense" reads (Section 14).       |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[param.<name>]        |One setting of the device (Section 15).     |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   |[signal.<name>]       |One thing the device reports (Section 16).  |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+

                      Table 2: Top-Level Tables

   A descriptor MUST declare at least one serve mode, and it MUST
   declare at least one bus or one relay: a daemon that neither
   translates nor relays has nothing to do.  A key that names another
   object MUST name one that is declared; a reference to nothing is a
   load-time error and not a run-time surprise.  Three keys, the "bus"
   of a codec, of an instruction and of an actuator, default to the
   first bus declared; a descriptor SHOULD state the bus rather than
   rely on the order of the tables.

   The descriptor is found by, in decreasing priority:

   1.  an explicit path given to the daemon;

   2.  the environment variable OCTO_DESCRIPTOR;

   3.  a file named "octo.toml" or "*.octo.toml" in the working
       directory;

   4.  a file named "octo.toml" or "*.octo.toml" below the user
       configuration directory;

   5.  a default packaged with the implementation.

   The recommended file name extension is ".octo.toml".  A file so named
   is recognised wherever this document is searched for; "octo.toml"
   remains accepted for compatibility.

   A missing file and a malformed document are both fatal and both name
   the path (Appendix A).  A descriptor is applied as a whole: a daemon
   MUST NOT start with a part of one.

   A conforming implementation reads a descriptor of format 1, thez
   version defined here.  A descriptor whose "format" is a value it does
   not implement MUST be rejected and MUST NOT be guessed at.

   |Key          |Type          |Meaning                               |

   |format       |integer       |REQUIRED.  The version of the format; |
   |             |              |1 in this document.                   |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   |id           |string        |OPTIONAL.  A stable identity of the   |

                   Table 3: Keys of the Meta Table

   An implementation MUST NOT infer a device identity from a name: "id"

      name   = "OctoBot 001"
      memo   = "A robot octopus."

5.  Session Tables

   [session.<name>] declares one variant of [RFC0004]: what it offers,
   what it requires and what carries it.  Every serve mode names one,
   and a session named "default" is assumed when a key names a session
   that is not declared: it offers the defaults of Section 5.1, has no
   authentication, no secure transport and no requirement.

5.1.  Offers

   The top-level offers of [RFC0004] are the keys of Table 4: what this
   endpoint is willing to use.  Each array-valued key is an array of
   algorithm names, most preferred first, as [RFC0004], Section 3.6,
   requires of an offer.  [RFC0009] and [RFC0010] are not part of the
   default configuration; their offers live in the "ocpfls" sub-table
   below and the "ocps" sub-table of Section 5.3.

   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | Key         | Type         | Meaning                              |
   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | comp        | array, text  | Compression algorithms.  OFF by        |
   |             |              | default unless at least one is         |
   |             |              | offered; an empty array offers         |
   |             |              | none.                                  |   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

                Table 4: Top-Level Offers (RFC0004 Defaults)

   The [session.<name>.ocpfls] sub-table holds the [RFC0009] frame-level
   security offers.  Its keys are listed in Table 5.

   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | Key         | Type         | Meaning                              |
   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | ocpfls.enc  | array, text  | Encryption algorithms of [RFC0009].  |
   |             |              | Default empty: encryption is not     |
   |             |              | offered.                             |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | ocpfls.itg  | array, text  | Integrity algorithms of [RFC0009].   |
   |             |              | Default empty.                       |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | ocpfls.ztr  | array, text  | Zero-trust algorithms of [RFC0009].  |
   |             |              | Default empty.                       |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

              Table 5: Keys of the [session.*.ocpfls] Sub-table

   The rules of the exchange are those of [RFC0004], Section 6.1, and
   of [RFC0009], Section 9.  Three of them are restated here because a
   descriptor is where they are decided.

   *  A primitive is never switched on by silence.  An absent or empty
      comp, ocpfls.enc, ocpfls.itg or ocpfls.ztr offers nothing.

   *  A session that offers ocpfls.enc or ocpfls.ztr MUST declare
      authentication with a secret (Section 5.2), or a method that
      yields one, because a primitive that has no key produces a
      session that fails on its first protected frame.

   The names are compared as exact strings and are not registered; a
   name an endpoint does not implement MUST be ignored in an offer and
   MUST NOT be confirmed, as [RFC0004], Section 3.6, requires.

   The transport protocol of a session is fixed by which offer sub-table
   it sets; ocpfls and ocps are mutually exclusive, and a descriptor
   that sets both is an error.  With neither, the session speaks the
   cleartext OCP of [RFC0004], Section 7.4 (ocp://, port 6286).  With
   ocpfls it speaks the frame-level secure transport of [RFC0009]
   (ocp-fls://, port 6357).  With ocps it speaks the secure transport
   of [RFC0010] (ocps://, port 16286, ALPN "ocp/1").  Setting either
   one therefore changes the wire protocol, not merely a parameter.

5.2.  Authentication

   The [session.<name>.auth] table is OPTIONAL.  Its keys are listed in
   Table 6; every other key of the table is defined by the method and is
   opaque to this document, exactly as the "auth" member of HELLO is
   opaque to [RFC0004], Section 6.2.

   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | Key         | Type         | Meaning                              |
   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | method      | string       | REQUIRED.  The authentication        |
   |             |              | method, named as [RFC0004], Section  |
   |             |              | 6.2, names one.  Compared exactly.   |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | secret      | string       | OPTIONAL.  The secret, written in    |
   |             |              | the descriptor.  Discouraged         |
   |             |              | (Section 19).                        |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | secret_env  | string       | OPTIONAL.  The name of an            |
   |             |              | environment variable that holds      |
   |             |              | the secret.                          |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

                 Table 6: Keys of the Authentication Table

   At most one of "secret" and "secret_env" MAY be given.  A method
   that needs no secret is given neither.  When a session is
   authenticated, the keys of [RFC0009] are derived from the secret the
   method yields or from this one, as [RFC0009], Section 7.1, states.

   The choice of method belongs to the initiator and is not negotiated.
   A descriptor therefore states what this endpoint accepts when it is
   the responder and what it offers when it is the initiator; a method
   this implementation does not implement is an error of the
   descriptor.

5.3.  Secure Transport

   The [session.<name>.ocps] table (the secure-transport sub-table) is
   OPTIONAL and selects the binding of [RFC0010]; the keys are in
   Table 7.

   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | Key         | Type         | Meaning                              |
   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | kind        | string       | "none", "tls", "dtls" or "quic".     |
   |             |              | Default "none": the session is       |
   |             |              | carried by the transport of          |
   |             |              | [RFC0004].                           |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | alpn        | string       | The ALPN identifier of [RFC0010],    |
   |             |              | Section 4.2.  Default "ocp/1".       |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | ca          | string       | OPTIONAL.  Path to the trust         |
   |             |              | anchors the peer is validated        |
   |             |              | against.                             |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | pin         | string       | OPTIONAL.  The pin of the peer:      |
   |             |              | "sha256:" followed by the hex        |
   |             |              | digits of the hash of its public     |
   |             |              | key or of its certificate.           |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | cert        | string       | OPTIONAL.  Path to the certificate   |
   |             |              | this endpoint presents as a client.  |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | key         | string       | OPTIONAL.  Path to the private key   |
   |             |              | of "cert".                           |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | sni         | string       | OPTIONAL.  The name sent in the      |
   |             |              | Server Name Indication.  Default     |
   |             |              | "host".                              |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | min_version | string       | The lowest version of the transport  |
   |             |              | that is accepted: "1.2" or "1.3".    |
   |             |              | Default "1.2".                       |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

                  Table 7: Keys of the [session.*.ocps] Sub-table

   The peer is always validated, as [RFC0010], Section 4.4, requires;
   this document defines no key that turns validation off.  A value of
   "min_version" below "1.2" MUST be rejected, and so must a value that
   names a version this implementation does not implement.

   A session that uses a secure transport does not use [RFC0009]:
   ocpfls.enc, ocpfls.itg, ocpfls.ztr MUST be empty when "kind" is not
   "none", and a descriptor that offers both is an error.  The two are
   alternatives, as [RFC0010], Section 1.1, states, and a descriptor
   that asks for both has not answered the question of which one
   protects the session.

5.4.  Requirements

   The [session.<name>.require] table is OPTIONAL.  Its keys are listed
   in Table 8; each is a boolean and each is false by default.  A
   requirement says what a session that does not meet it does.

   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | Key         | Type         | Meaning                              |
   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | auth        | boolean      | Refuse a session that is not         |
   |             |              | authenticated.                       |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | comp        | boolean      | Refuse a session on which no         |
   |             |              | compression algorithm was agreed.    |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | ocpfls.enc  | boolean      | Refuse a session on which            |
   |             |              | encryption was not agreed.           |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | ocpfls.itg  | boolean      | Refuse a session on which no         |
   |             |              | integrity algorithm was agreed.      |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | ocpfls.ztr  | boolean      | Refuse a session on which no zero-   |
   |             |              | trust algorithm was agreed.          |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

                 Table 8: Keys of the Requirement Table

   The [RFC0009] primitives are reached through the ocpfls sub-table,
   so a requirement for one is written ocpfls.enc, ocpfls.itg or
   ocpfls.ztr.

   A session that does not meet a requirement is refused, and is
   refused rather than served without what was required: an
   unauthenticated session is worse than no session.  As [RFC0009],
   Section 11, states, an endpoint that is configured to require a
   primitive and cannot negotiate it closes the session instead of
   serving it in cleartext.

   A requirement MUST be satisfiable by what the same session offers:
   requiring a primitive that is not offered, requiring "auth" without
   an "auth" table, or requiring ocpfls.enc or ocpfls.ztr without a
   secret, is an error of the descriptor.

6.  Serve Tables

   [serve.<name>] declares one serve mode: one way the device is
   offered, as an independent listener that binds its own host and
   port and speaks its own variant of [RFC0004].  Each
   [serve.<name>] table is a sub-configuration of the descriptor;
   several MAY be declared, and the daemon opens them all in
   parallel, each serving the same device
   from
   the same translation.  The keys are listed in Table 9.

   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | Key         | Type         | Meaning                              |
   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | host        | string       | The address to bind: an IPv4 or      |
   |             |              | IPv6 address, or a host name.  An    |
   |             |              | IPv6 literal is written bare, without|
   |             |              | brackets, because "port" is a        |
   |             |              | separate key; "::" binds every IPv6  |
   |             |              | interface.  Default "127.0.0.1".     |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | port        | integer      | Default 6286, the [RFC0004] port      |
   |             |              | by [RFC0004], Section 7.4; 6357 with  |
   |             |              | [RFC0009] (ocp-fls://) and 16286 with |
   |             |              | [RFC0010] (ocps://), per the session  |
   |             |              | named by this serve mode.             |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | session     | string       | The variant of [RFC0004] spoken      |
   |             |              | here.  Default "default".            |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | expose      | array, text  | OPTIONAL.  The names this serve mode |
   |             |              | offers; default all of them.  A      |
   |             |              | name MUST be one that is declared    |
   |             |              | (Sections 12 to 16).                 |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | caps        | array, text  | The capabilities of [RFC0004],       |
   |             |              | Section 8.  Default ["rpc",          |
   |             |              | "pubsub", "blobtun"].                |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | max_payload | integer      | Largest payload accepted, in octets. |
   |             |              | Default 1048576.                     |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | window      | integer      | Tunnel receive window, in octets.    |
   |             |              | Default 65536.                       |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | slice       | integer      | Tunnel slice size, in octets.        |
   |             |              | Default 8192.                        |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | buffer      | integer      | Bound on received but unconsumed     |
   |             |              | tunnel octets.  Default 262144.      |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | heartbeat   | float        | Seconds between heartbeat calls; 0   |
   |             |              | disables them.  Default 5.0.         |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | liveness    | float        | Seconds without an answer before a   |
   |             |              | caller is considered lost.  Default  |
   |             |              | 15.0.                                |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | enabled     | boolean      | Default true.  When false the serve  |
   |             |              | mode is declared but not opened.     |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

                    Table 9: Keys of a Serve Table

   The numbers of Table 9 are the recommendations of [RFC0004],
   Sections 3.3, 6.6.7 and 6.7, restated as defaults; a deployment MAY
   change any of them and none of them is negotiated by this document.

   Two serve modes MUST NOT share a host and a port.  What they differ
   in is where the device is offered and which variant of [RFC0004] is
   spoken there, and optionally in which part of the translation is
   offered: a name that a serve mode does not expose is unknown to a
   caller on it, and is answered with an application error in RESULT, as
   [RFC0004], Section 6.4.2, requires of any name that is not known.
   The translation itself is shared and is never declared twice, so two
   serve modes cannot disagree about what a call does.

7.  Access Tables

   Access is controlled by whitelist and blacklist entries.  A whitelist
   entry names an address that is allowed; a blacklist entry names an
   address that is denied.  Both kinds share the keys of Table 10.

   An entry is declared at one of two levels.  At the top level,
   [[whitelist]] or [[blacklist]] is an array of entries that applies
   to every serve mode.  Nested under a serve, an
   [[serve.<name>.whitelist]] or [[serve.<name>.blacklist]]
   applies only to that serve mode.
   Each entry carries an "addr" that is a CIDR range, in IPv4 or
   IPv6, such as "192.168.1.0/24" or "2001:db8::/32", or a bare
   single address such as "10.0.0.9" (that host, /32) or
   "2001:db8::1" (/128), and an optional "memo".

   The two kinds are mutually exclusive at one level: a level
   MUST NOT declare both a whitelist entry and a blacklist entry.
   The inner level MUST declare the same kind as the outer level when
   the outer level declares one; an inner blacklist under an outer
   whitelist, or the reverse, is a load-time error.  When the outer
   level declares no entry, the inner level MAY declare either kind.

   The effective allow set of a serve mode is the intersection of the
   outer allow set and the inner allow set.  The outer allow set is
   the addresses an outer whitelist names, or every address an outer
   blacklist does not name, or every address when the outer level is
   silent; the inner allow set is the same per serve mode.  A caller
   whose address is in the intersection is allowed; one outside it is
   refused before authentication.  When both levels are silent, every
   allowed: silence is not a denial.

   | Key         | Type         | Meaning                              |
   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | addr        | string       | REQUIRED.  A CIDR range in IPv4 or   |
   |             |              | IPv6, such as "192.168.1.0/24" or    |
   |             |              | "2001:db8::/32", or a bare single    |
   |             |              | address such as "10.0.0.9" (that     |
   |             |              | host, /32) or "2001:db8::1" (/128).  |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | memo        | string       | OPTIONAL.  A description meant for   |

8.  Relay Tables

   [relay.<name>] declares one relay: calls it takes are passed on
   unchanged to a peer at the address it names.  A relay translates
   nothing: it is the case where there is nothing to translate, because
   the peer already speaks [RFC0004].  The keys are listed in Table 11.

   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | Key         | Type         | Meaning                              |
   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | host        | string       | REQUIRED.  The address of the peer   |
   |             |              | the relay reaches: an IPv4 or IPv6   |
   |             |              | address, or a host name.             |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | port        | integer      | Default 6286, or 6357 with           |
   |             |              | [RFC0009] (ocp-fls://) and 16286     |
   |             |              | with [RFC0010] (ocps://), per the    |
   |             |              | session of this relay.               |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | session     | string       | The variant of [RFC0004] spoken to   |
   |             |              | the peer.  Default "default".        |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | timeout     | float        | Seconds to wait for WELCOME.         |
   |             |              | Default 3.0.                         |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | retry       | float        | Seconds between attempts; 0 gives    |
   |             |              | up after one.  Default 5.0.          |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | prefix      | string       | OPTIONAL.  A namespace: a call whose |
   |             |              | name begins with it is relayed with  |
   |             |              | the prefix taken off.                |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | events      | boolean      | Default true.  Republish what the    |
   |             |              | peer publishes (below).              |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | subscribe   | boolean      | Default true.  Forward a             |
   |             |              | subscription this daemon does not    |
   |             |              | publish itself (below).              |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | memo        | string       | OPTIONAL.  A description meant for   |
   |             |              | a human.                             |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

                      Table 11: Keys of a Relay Table

   At most one relay MAY omit "prefix".  That relay takes every name
   that this descriptor does not translate; a relay with a prefix takes
   the names that begin with it.  A name is either translated here or
   relayed, and never both:

   *  A declared instruction (Section 12) MUST NOT begin with the
      prefix of a relay.  Such a declaration is an error of the
      descriptor.

   *  A call that no relay takes and that no instruction declares is
      unknown, and is answered with an application error in RESULT.

   A relayed call is sent as a CALL of [RFC0004] with the name the
   prefix leaves, with the arguments of the call unchanged and with
   nothing decoded, re-encoded or renamed on the way.  The value of the
   RESULT is returned unchanged, and an application error from the peer
   is returned unchanged likewise.  "sense", "actuate", "get_param",
   "set_param" and the rest of the core profile (Section 18.1) are never
   relayed: they are answered here.

   What a relay publishes and forwards:

   *  With "events" set, what the peer publishes is republished on the
      sessions of this daemon: with a prefix, under the topic the
      prefix and the topic of the peer make when they are put together;
      without one, under the topic itself.  A topic that this daemon
      publishes itself is not republished.

   *  With "subscribe" set, a subscription to a topic that this daemon
      does not publish is forwarded to the peer that publishes it, and
      the answer of the peer is returned.

   A relay has no bus, no codec and no arguments, and no table of this
   document other than this one applies to it.

9.  Bus Tables

   [bus.<name>] declares one hardware bus: a carrier of frames between
   the daemon and a device, whose octets this descriptor encodes and
   decodes.  A bus is never visible to a caller.  The keys are listed in
   Table 12.

   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | Key         | Type         | Meaning                              |
   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | transport   | string       | REQUIRED.  The name of a bus         |
   |             |              | transport.                           |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | mtu         | integer      | The largest number of data octets    |
   |             |              | one frame carries.  Default 8.       |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | timeout     | float        | Seconds to wait for a frame that     |
   |             |              | answers one that was sent. Default   |
   |             |              | 3.0.                                 |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | enabled     | boolean      | Default true.  When false the bus    |
   |             |              | is declared but not opened.          |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

                       Table 12: Keys of a Bus Table

   The [bus.<name>.options] table is OPTIONAL.  Its keys are defined by
   the transport named by "transport" and are passed to it unchanged,
   so that a transport gains settings without a change to this
   document; a transport that is given a key it does not define rejects
   the descriptor.

   The transport name "loopback" MUST be implemented by every
   conforming implementation.  It retains every frame that is sent so
   that it can be received again, touches no hardware, and is the means
   of exercising a descriptor without a device.  Every other name is
   implementation-defined and is discoverable from the command line
   [RFC0007]; a name this implementation does not implement is an error
   of the descriptor.

   A bus exists so that a device that cannot speak [RFC0004] can still
   be served: hardware that predates the protocol, a machine that will
   never be updated, a link that is a link and not a computer.  A device
   that does speak it is a peer and is relayed (Section 8), and no bus
   is declared for it.

   A field carries a value of one of three kinds, and which one is
   decided by its type and by its "scale" and "bias".  A field of an
   integer type whose "scale" and "bias" are their defaults carries an
   integer of that type.  Every other numeric field carries a float: a
   float field carries one, and so does an integer field that is scaled
   or biased, because what a program sees is the physical value and not
   the raw one.  A field of type "bytes" carries a byte string.  This is
   the value type of the field, and it is what the type of an argument
   (Section 12), of a sensor (Section 14) and of a parameter
   (Section 15) is checked against.

   The layout rules are:

   *  A field MUST NOT begin before zero and MUST NOT extend past the
      "mtu" of its bus.  A field that does is an error of the
      descriptor, and so is a "len" larger than the "mtu".

   *  Two fields MUST NOT share an octet.  A gap is allowed and the
      octets of a gap are zero when encoding and ignored when decoding.

   *  A field of type "bytes" whose "const" is given MUST have "count"
      equal to the number of octets the literal writes.

      [codec.drive]
      bus  = "main"
      addr = 0x123
      len  = 8

      [[codec.drive.field]]
      name  = "cmd"
      type  = "u8"
      const = 0xA1

      [[codec.drive.field]]
      name  = "speed"
      type  = "i16"
      scale = 0.01
      unit  = "rad/s"
      range = [-31.0, 31.0]

      [[codec.drive.field]]
      name    = "mode"
      type    = "u8"
      enum    = "mode"
      default = 1

   The codec above is eight data octets wide: one for "cmd", two for
   "speed" and one for "mode", with four octets of gap that are written
   as zero because "len" is 8.  A caller that names no "mode" gets 1,
   which the enumeration calls "run".

11.2.  Encoding

   Encoding renders a codec and a set of bound values into the data
   octets of a frame.  A value is bound to a field by its name: by an
   argument of an instruction (Section 12) or of an actuator
   (Section 13), by the value a parameter is written with (Section 15)
   or by the "default" of the field.  Every field that has a name MUST
   be bound, and a binding that names no field is an error of the
   descriptor.

   For each field, in order:

   1.  The physical value is taken from the binding, or from "default"
       when the binding supplies none.  A field with a name that is
       neither bound nor has a "default" is an error of the descriptor.

   2.  When "enum" is given and the value is a string, the value is the
       integer the enumeration maps it to.  A string the enumeration
       does not name is an error of the call.

   3.  When "range" is given, the physical value MUST lie within it.  A
       value that does not is an error of the call, unless "saturate" is
       true, in which case the value is clamped to the nearer end.

   4.  The raw value is the physical value minus "bias", divided by
       "scale", rounded to the nearest integer for an integer type.  A
       raw value that does not fit the width of the type is an error of
       the call.

   5.  The raw value is written at "at" in the order named by "endian".
       A "const" field is written from its literal and takes no
       binding.  The octets of a gap and the octets past the last field
       up to "len" are zero.

   Constant fields are what make a codec a template rather than a
   record: they are the parts of a frame that a caller never chooses.

11.3.  Decoding and Matching

   A frame that a bus receives is offered to the codecs of that bus, in
   the order they are declared.  A codec matches when all of the
   following hold:

   1.  The address of the frame equals "addr".

   2.  The data is at least as long as the width of the codec, and,
       when "len" is declared, exactly as long.

   3.  Every field with a "const" is equal to its literal.

   The first codec that matches decodes the frame and no other codec is
   tried.  For each field, in order, the raw value is read at "at" in
   the order named by "endian"; the physical value is the raw value
   multiplied by "scale" plus "bias"; when "enum" is given and the
   enumeration names the result, the value is that name.  A value is
   reported under the name of its field, and the octets of a gap are
   discarded.

   Decoding applies no "range": a range is a guard on what this daemon
   sends and not a filter on what a device reports, and a device that
   reports a value outside one is still understood.

   A frame that matches no codec is discarded.  A bus is the daemon's
   own business and is not published: a caller sees devices, not
   frames, and a frame that nothing understands is reported by the
   device that owns it or is not reported at all.

   member of CALL ([RFC0004], Section 5.3), and it MUST NOT be one of
   the reserved names of Section 18.1 or the name of an actuator
   (Section 13).

   The instruction renders "req" with the arguments of the call and
   writes it to the bus.  The rules of binding are those of
   Section 11.2, with three additions:

   *  Every argument MUST bind to a field of "req" by name, and every
      named field of "req" MUST be bound by an argument, by its
      "default" or by "const".  A mismatch is an error of the
      descriptor, which is what makes metadata truthful.

   *  The type of an argument MUST be the value type of the field it
      binds to (Section 11.1).

   *  When "res" is given, the call does not complete until a frame
      matching that codec arrives on the same bus, and returns the
      values of that codec.  When it is not given, the call completes
      when the frame has been written and returns null.  A call that
      waits and is not answered within "timeout" fails.

   answered in RESULT, as [RFC0004], Section 6.4.2, requires: an
   argument that does not fit, a value outside a range, a bus that
   cannot be written to and a reply that never arrived are all failures
   of one call and leave the session usable.

      [instruction.drive]
      bus  = "main"
      req  = "drive"
      memo = "Spin a joint at a speed."

      [[instruction.drive.arg]]

      type  = "f32"
      unit  = "rad/s"
      range = [-31.0, 31.0]

   |             | float        | reading is expected to lie between.  |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | enum        | string       | OPTIONAL.  The name of an            |
   |             |              | enumeration.                         |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | default     | value        | OPTIONAL.  The value held before     |
   |             |              | anything arrives.  Default zero.     |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | codec       | string       | OPTIONAL.  The codec whose decoded   |
   |             |              | frame carries the reading.           |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | field       | string       | REQUIRED when "codec" is given: the  |
   |             |              | name of the field that carries the   |
   |             |              | reading.                             |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | read        | table        | OPTIONAL.  How a fresh reading is    |
   |             |              | asked for (Table 22).                |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | memo        | string       | OPTIONAL.  A description meant for   |
   |             |              | a human.                             |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

                       Table 20: Keys of a Sensor

   At least one of "codec" and "read" MUST be given.  The "field" of a
   codec MUST name a field of it whose value type (Section 11.1) is the
   type of the sensor.

   A sensor is a measurement and not a setting: it is read-only, and no
   instruction of [RFC0004], Section 9.1, writes one.  "sense" returns
   the most recent reading the daemon holds.  When "read" is declared,
   "sense" sends the request first, and waits when "block" is true; a
   sensor with no "read" is fed by whatever the device sends, and a
   reading that arrives unbidden updates it just the same.

   A sensor is a state and not a fact, so it is not published: a
   program that wants to follow one asks for it.  What the device
   publishes is events (Section 16).

15.  Parameter Tables

   [param.<name>] declares one setting of the device, which a program
   reads and writes with the core instructions "get_param" and
   "set_param" (Section 18.2).  Its keys are listed in Table 22.

   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | Key         | Type         | Meaning                              |
   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | bus         | string       | The bus that carries the value.      |
   |             |              | Default the first bus declared.      |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | type        | string       | REQUIRED.  The type of the value     |
   |             |              | (Section 11.1).                      |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | unit        | string       | OPTIONAL.  The unit of the value.    |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | range       | array,       | OPTIONAL.  Two numbers, checked      |
   |             | float        | when the value is written.           |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | enum        | string       | OPTIONAL.  The name of an            |
   |             |              | enumeration.                         |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | default     | value        | OPTIONAL.  The value held before     |
   |             |              | anything is read.  Default zero.     |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | read        | table        | OPTIONAL.  How the value is read     |
   |             |              | (Table 22).                          |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | write       | table        | OPTIONAL.  How the value is written  |
   |             |              | (Table 22).                          |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | memo        | string       | OPTIONAL.  A description meant for   |
   |             |              | a human.                             |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

                    Table 21: Keys of a Parameter

   At least one of "read" and "write" MUST be given; a parameter that
   can be neither is a name and not a value.  Giving "read" alone makes
   the parameter read-only, giving "write" alone makes it write-only
   and giving both makes it read-write.  This mode is the numeric
   "mode" member the peer reports for the parameter in metadata: 4
   for read-only, 6 for read-write and 2 for write-only, in Linux
   permission notation with the x bit unused, so a caller learns it
   at the application layer.

   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | Key         | Type         | Meaning                              |
   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | req         | string       | REQUIRED.  The codec of the frame    |
   |             |              | sent.                                |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | res         | string       | The codec of the frame that          |
   |             |              | answers.  REQUIRED for "read",       |
   |             |              | OPTIONAL for "write".                |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | field       | string       | REQUIRED.  The name of the field,    |
   |             |              | of "req" for a write and of "res"    |
   |             |              | for a read, that carries the value.  |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | block       | boolean      | For "read" only.  Default false.     |
   |             |              | When true the call waits for the     |
   |             |              | answer instead of returning what     |
   |             |              | is cached.                           |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | timeout     | float        | Seconds to wait.  Default the        |
   |             |              | "timeout" of the bus.                |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

                Table 22: Keys of a Read or a Write

   The "field" of a read or a write MUST name a field of the codec it
   names, and the value type of that field (Section 11.1) MUST be the
   type of the parameter.  Every other field of "req" is bound as in
   Section 11.2, from its "default" or from its "const".

   A read sends "req" and answers with the value the daemon holds.  It
   does not block unless "block" is true, so a program that polls
   neither waits for the bus nor holds up the session; the value is
   replaced whenever a frame matching "res" arrives, whether the frame
   was asked for or not.  A write sends "req" with the value bound to
   "field" and sets the value the daemon holds to what was written;
   when "res" is given it waits for the answer and fails on timeout.

   A parameter is a cache and not a promise: what a read returns is the
   most recent value this daemon has seen, and a program that needs a
   fresh one asks for it with "block" set to true.  Whenever the value
   changes, the daemon publishes it (Section 18.3).

      [param.stiffness]
      bus   = "main"
      type  = "f32"
      unit  = "N*m/rad"
      range = [0.0, 200.0]
      read  = { req = "p_read", res = "p_read_res",
                field = "value" }
      write = { req = "p_write", field = "value" }

16.  Signal Tables

   [signal.<name>] declares one thing the device reports: the frame that
   carries it and what it means.  Its keys are listed in Table 23.

   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | Key         | Type         | Meaning                              |
   +=============+==============+======================================+
   | codec       | string       | REQUIRED.  The codec that decodes    |
   |             |              | the frame.  The bus of the signal is |
   |             |              | the bus of that codec.               |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | fault       | boolean      | Default false.  When true the signal |
   |             |              | is a fault: it is published as one   |
   |             |              | and is latched until it is cleared.  |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | clears      | string       | OPTIONAL.  The name of another       |
   |             |              | signal: when this one is reported,   |
   |             |              | the fault of that name is cleared.   |
   |             |              | The named signal MUST have "fault"   |
   |             |              | set to true.                         |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | edge        | boolean      | Default true.  When true, a frame    |
   |             |              | whose values are the same as those   |
   |             |              | of the last frame reported is not    |
   |             |              | reported again.                      |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | memo        | string       | OPTIONAL.  A description meant for   |
   |             |              | a human.                             |
   +-------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

                       Table 23: Keys of a Signal

   A frame is decoded once, by the first codec that matches it
   (Section 11.3).  When a signal names that codec, the values are
   published under the name of the signal (Section 18.3); when several
   signals name it, they are published once per signal, because one
   frame may mean two things at once.

   A fault is latched.  Once one is published it is not published again
   until it is cleared, and it is cleared either by a call of
   "device.clear_fault" or by the signal named by "clears"; when it is
   cleared, the daemon publishes the fact.  A consumer SHOULD hold a
   fault until it sees it cleared.

   "edge" is what keeps a status frame that a device sends a hundred
   times a second from becoming a hundred events a second: only a change
   is reported.  A consumer that wants the present state asks for it
   with a call instead, which is what a sensor and a parameter are for.

17.  Validation

   A descriptor is validated before any of it is applied, in the
   following order.  A later phase is not begun while an earlier one has
   findings that make it meaningless.

   1.  Read and parse.  The file must exist, be readable and be valid
       TOML, and its "format" must be one this implementation reads.

   2.  Structure.  Every key is one the table defines; every value is of
       the type the table states; every required key is present; every
       name is a valid name; no two objects of one kind share a name.

   3.  References.  Every name that a key names is declared and is of
       the kind the key wants: a serve mode names a session, a codec
       names a bus, an instruction and an
       actuator name a codec, a signal names a codec, a field names an
       enumeration.

   4.  Meaning.  The rules of the tables themselves: a field fits the
       "mtu" and shares no octet; an argument binds to a field of its
       own value type (Section 11.1); a session offers what it
       requires and does not offer [RFC0009] together with [RFC0010];
       an instruction does not begin with the prefix of a relay; at
       most one relay omits a prefix; a name exposed by a serve mode is
       one that is declared.

   5.  Compile.  Every codec is compiled: the offsets are resolved, the
       widths are fixed and the defaults are checked against their
       types.

   An implementation SHOULD report every finding and not only the first,
   because a descriptor is written by a human who would rather fix six
   things at once.  An implementation MUST NOT start with a descriptor
   that has a finding and MUST NOT apply a part of one: a half-applied
   descriptor is a device whose behaviour nobody has written down.

   A diagnostic is written as

      <file>: <path>: <message>

   where <file> is the path of the descriptor, <path> is the key path of
   the table or key that is at fault and <message> is one of Appendix A.
   All of them are in English.

   These are the diagnostics of loading a descriptor, and they are the
   only diagnostics this document defines.  What a running daemon
   reports, and how a failure of one is classified, belong to the
   taxonomy of [RFC0008]; the tools that read and check a descriptor
   belong to [RFC0007].

18.  Publication

18.1.  Reserved Names

   The names of the core profile of [RFC0004], Section 9.1 -- "ping",
   "status", "metadata", "sense", "actuate", "get_param" and
   "set_param" -- are reserved, and so is every name that begins with
   "device." or with "core.".  A descriptor MUST NOT declare an
   instruction, an actuator, a sensor or a parameter with a reserved
   name; such a declaration is an error of the descriptor.  A sensor and
   a parameter share no namespace with an instruction, but the reserved
   names cover all of them.

18.2.  What the Core Instructions Do

   The daemon answers the core profile of [RFC0004], Section 9.1, from
   what the descriptor declares.

   *  "metadata" reports what the device offers.  Its "instructions"
      member carries the instructions of Section 12, its "actuators"
      member the actuators of Section 13, its "sensors" member the
      sensors of Section 14, its "params" member the parameters of
      Section 15 and its "events" member the signals of Section 16,
      each under the name that is declared for it and with the "unit",
      "range", "default", "mode" and "memo" that are declared for
      it.  A serve
      mode that exposes a part of the translation reports that part and
      not the whole.

   *  "sense" reads the sensor its "name" argument names and returns
      the reading (Section 14).

   *  "actuate" operates the actuator its "name" argument names, with
      the arguments that follow bound to the fields of its "req" as in
      Section 11.2 (Section 13).

   *  "get_param" and "set_param" read and write the parameter their
      "name" argument names (Section 15).

   *  "status" answers the state of the device: "fault" when a fault is
      latched (Section 16), and then with the name of that fault;
      "offline" when no bus it needs is open; "ready" otherwise.  Its
      "time" and "uptime" members are the daemon's own.

   *  "device.clear_fault" clears the latched fault that its "name"
      argument names (Section 16).  It is provided by the daemon, under
      the reserved prefix, and is not part of the core profile: a relay
      never takes it.

   *  "ping" is answered as [RFC0004], Section 6.7, requires.

   A call that names something a serve mode does not expose, and a call
   that names something that is not declared, are answered with an
   application error in RESULT and never with ERROR, as [RFC0004],
   Section 6.4.2, requires of any name that is not known.  Nothing in
   this document answers a call by acting on its own: the only thing
   that acts is a caller.

   Publishing is best effort and an event is a fact and not a state, as

19.  Security Considerations

   *  A descriptor is trusted input.  It decides what an instruction
      does to a machine, and it can cause arbitrary frames to be
      written to hardware.  It MUST NOT be accepted from a peer, from a
      session or from anything else that is not the operator, and it
      SHOULD be readable only by the account that runs the daemon.

   *  A secret in the descriptor is a secret in a file.  "secret_env" is
      preferred over "secret" because an environment variable is not
      copied into a backup, a bug report or a repository.  A secret MUST
      NOT be written to a log, MUST NOT be returned by an instruction
      and MUST NOT appear in a diagnostic.

   *  A requirement is a refusal and not a preference.  An endpoint that
      requires authentication, or a primitive of [RFC0009], closes a
      session that does not meet it rather than serving it without, and
      an implementation MUST NOT offer a way to lower one at run time.

   *  The two protections are alternatives.  A descriptor that offers
      both a secure transport and a primitive of [RFC0009] is rejected
      (Section 5.3), because a session protected twice hides nothing
      extra and leaves two places to make one mistake.

   *  A range is a guard and not an interlock.  It stops a caller from
      asking for a value the field cannot carry; it does not stop a
      machine from being told to move, and it is not a safety function.
      A deployment that needs one puts it where it cannot be turned off
      by editing a file.

   *  A serve mode SHOULD bind the loopback address (IPv4 127.0.0.1 or
      IPv6 ::1) unless remote access
      is intended, and a serve mode that is reachable SHOULD be
      authenticated.  A session that is not authenticated and is not
      carried by a secure transport is cleartext, as [RFC0004], Section
      12, warns.

   *  A relay passes a call on unchanged and applies no filtering of its
      own: what the peer accepts is what it declares, and authorisation
      belongs to the peer.  A relay is a way to reach a device and not
      a way to limit what a caller may ask of it.

   *  A descriptor contains no control logic, and that is a property
      worth keeping: nothing in this format acts on what a device
      reports, so the only thing that can move a machine through a
      descriptor is a caller on the other side of a session, which can
      be authenticated, logged and refused.  A deployment that wants a
      device to act on its own writes a program, and puts it where it
      can be reviewed.

20.  IANA Considerations

   This document has no IANA actions.  It requests no name, no number
   and no code point, and it establishes no registry administered by
   IANA.

   The namespaces this document defines are maintained by this series,
   and a value is added to one of them only by a document of the series:

   *  The keys of every table of the format.

   *  The field types of Table 16.

   *  The reserved names of Section 18.1.

   One namespace is not maintained at all.  A bus transport name other
   than "loopback" is chosen by the implementation that provides it,
   carries no meaning beyond that implementation and is never
   registered.

   A caller on the local address is authenticated and sees the part of
   the translation that "expose" names; a caller on the remote address
   sees the whole device over TLS.  Both see the same translation, and
   neither sees the bus.  The outer whitelist allows "192.168.0.0/24" at
   every serve mode, and the remote mode adds an inner whitelist
   that narrows it to "192.168.0.50/32"; with none declared, every
   caller would be allowed.  A call to "tool.open" is relayed to the
   peer at the relay's address as "open", and what that peer publishes
   is republished under "tool.<topic>".
